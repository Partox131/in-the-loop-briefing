About In the Loop

In the Loop helps you stay on top of fast-moving stories without doom-scrolling. It follows credible outlets, reporters and analysts, groups what they publish into stories, and shows where each story stands: how many independent sources carry it, whether it's confirmed or disputed, and what changed since you last looked.

What we cover

Three segments today: geopolitics (the Middle East, Ukraine and NATO's eastern flank, China and Taiwan), US politics and Canadian politics (English-language sources for now). More topics come as we add sources.

Where the stories come from

  • 119 accounts on Bluesky: news outlets, reporters, analysts, officials and open-source investigators, each checked by hand. Each has a type and, for state-affiliated outlets, the state's flag.
  • 144 news outlets' RSS feeds, read every 10 to 15 minutes.
  • 31 public Telegram channels (armies, ministries and newsrooms), where official statements often appear first. We show their headline and a link only.

We don't post, like or follow anything on any network. We only read public posts and feeds.

How a story is built

When several outlets report the same event, we join their posts and articles into one story, using shared links, quotes and similar wording. The headline is the first sentence of the earliest report. There is no AI on the site: grouping, counts and labels are rules you can read below.

What the labels mean

  • Confirmed: carried by at least two independent outlets or reporters.
  • Official: an official account (a government, army or agency) is a source.
  • Reported once: one source so far. Worth watching, not yet confirmed.
  • Sources disagree: credible sources contradict each other on the facts.
  • Breaking: a new event (a strike, an arrest, a resignation, a vote), reported by at least 2 independent sources within 30 minutes of the first report. It stays breaking for an hour. Statements, plans and old news are never breaking.
  • Hot: a story widely covered right now: at least 4 independent sources in the last 6 hours, or 3 while its topic is running at twice its usual pace.
  • Independent sources are outlets, reporters and neutral officials. Analysts, partisan and state-controlled outlets don't count, state media from one country count once, and a side's own claims or a disputed story are never breaking or hot.

Political lean

In the US and Canadian politics segments, each source carries a lean label (left, centre-left, centre, centre-right, right). These are our own first call, so you can see the mix behind a story at a glance. Bluesky leans left on North American politics, so we add right-of-centre outlets through their news feeds where we can.
