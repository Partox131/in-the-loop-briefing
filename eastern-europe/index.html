Russia's war on Ukraine · NATO's eastern flank

Why we're watching Eastern Europe

Europe's largest war since 1945 is in its fifth year. Russia invaded Ukraine in February 2022, eight years after it seized Crimea. The fighting has spilled into neighbouring NATO countries, which face stray drones and missiles, sabotage and cyberattacks, and are re-arming fast.

Russia's war on Ukraine

Since 24 February 2022

In 2014, after protests in Kyiv toppled a pro-Russian president, Russia seized and annexed Crimea and backed armed separatists in eastern Ukraine's Donbas region. Fighting there simmered for eight years.

On 24 February 2022 Russia launched a full-scale invasion from the north, east and south, including towards Kyiv. Ukraine pushed Russian forces back from the capital and later from parts of the north-east and south, and the war settled into a long, grinding fight along a front line in the east and south, with drone and missile strikes on cities far from it. In September 2022 Russia claimed to annex four Ukrainian regions it did not fully control.

What each side wants

  • Russia: At the start it called for Ukraine's 'demilitarisation' and 'denazification' without saying clearly what they meant. It now wants all occupied land, plus the parts of the four regions it claims but doesn't hold, recognised as Russian, a guarantee Ukraine never joins NATO, limits on Ukraine's army and sanctions lifted.
  • Ukraine: Russian forces out and its borders restored, security guarantees so it can't be invaded again, and prisoners and deported children returned. Since March 2025 it has offered an unconditional ceasefire, which Russia has refused.
  • US and Europe: Since 2025 the US has pushed for a negotiated deal; plans so far have been rejected by both sides. European countries back Ukraine and favour a ceasefire guarded by a 'coalition of the willing'.

Where it stands

There is no ceasefire. The UN says civilian deaths in 2026 already exceed all of 2025, and Russia stepped up strikes on Ukraine's power grid in October as winter approaches.

In numbers

  • ~18,000 civilians killed in Ukraine since Feb 2022, and almost 58,000 injured (verified minimum) (UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission, 17 November 2026)
  • 2,957 civilians killed Jan–Oct 2026, more than in all of 2025 (UN OHCHR, 16 November 2026)
  • ~1.5 million Russian soldiers killed or wounded, about 500,000 of them killed (estimate) (UK government, via Wikipedia, 11 September 2026)
  • 96,821 Ukrainian soldiers confirmed killed by name, plus 97,938 missing; the true toll is higher (UALosses project, via Wikipedia, 21 June 2026)

Timeline

  • 1 March 2014Russia annexes Crimea; war begins in Donbas weeks later.
  • 24 February 2022Full-scale invasion.
  • 29 March 2022Peace talks in Istanbul; they break down within weeks.
  • 30 September 2022Russia claims to annex Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson.
  • 1 January 2025Renewed US-led negotiations; no deal.
  • 2 November 2026A strike on a school in Komyshuvakha kills 72, per Ukrainian authorities, one of the deadliest attacks on civilians of the war.

NATO's eastern flank

Russia's invasion of Ukraine alarmed the countries along Russia's and Belarus's borders. Finland and Sweden, neutral for decades, joined NATO in 2023 and 2024. Poland and the Baltic states, which were under Moscow's control in the Soviet era, see themselves as next in line.

Since then the region has seen what officials call 'grey zone' attacks: damaged undersea cables and pipelines in the Baltic Sea, drones and missiles straying or sent into NATO airspace, sabotage on railways, spy arrests and cyberattacks. Each is below the level of open war but tests how NATO responds.

What each side wants

  • NATO's frontline states: Deter Russia by showing NATO will defend every member, and re-arm fast. Poland spent about 4.3% of its GDP on defence in 2025, more than double NATO's old 2% benchmark.
  • NATO: At The Hague in June 2025, allies agreed to spend 5% of GDP on defence and security by 2035, at least 3.5% on core defence.
  • Russia: Has long opposed NATO expanding towards its borders and calls the alliance a threat.

Where it stands

Incidents keep coming. In 2026 alone a cruise missile crossed into Polish airspace (July), a drone strayed into Latvia (August) and another was shot down over Lithuania (September), and NATO is building up air defences along the whole border.

In numbers

  • 18 Russian incursions into NATO airspace in the region in 2025 (Atlantic Council, 7 October 2026)
  • 5% of GDP: NATO allies' defence and security spending pledge for 2035 (NATO, The Hague Summit Declaration, 25 June 2025)

Timeline

  • 4 April 2023Finland joins NATO.
  • 7 March 2024Sweden joins NATO.
  • 1 December 2024The Estlink 2 power cable between Finland and Estonia is damaged; NATO starts Baltic Sentry patrols in January.
  • 10 September 2025Russian drones enter Polish airspace and are shot down; NATO launches Eastern Sentry.
  • 1 November 2025Sabotage on the Warsaw–Lublin railway, linked to Russian intelligence.
  • 1 September 2026NATO jets shoot down a drone over Lithuania.
