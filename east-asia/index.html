China and Taiwan

In 1949, at the end of China's civil war, the Communists proclaimed the People's Republic in Beijing and the defeated Republic of China government retreated to Taiwan. No peace treaty was ever signed. Taiwan has since become a democracy with its own government, army and elections; Beijing has never ruled it but says it is part of China.

The United States switched recognition to Beijing in 1979 but, under the Taiwan Relations Act, still supplies Taiwan with weapons to defend itself. Since Nancy Pelosi, then US House Speaker, visited Taipei in August 2022, China has staged a series of large drills around the island.

What each side wants

  • China: Unification. It prefers to achieve it peacefully under 'one country, two systems', but its 2005 Anti-Secession Law allows military force if Taiwan moves towards formal independence.
  • Taiwan: Keep its own government and way of life. President Lai Ching-te, in office since May 2024, rejects Beijing's claim and in 2025 called China a 'foreign hostile force'.
  • United States: Prevent a forced change to the status quo. It arms Taiwan but deliberately does not say whether it would fight for it ('strategic ambiguity').

Where it stands

In November 2026 Taiwan reported large, unannounced Chinese naval drills in the western Pacific, east of the island, which analysts read as practice for blocking ships from reaching Taiwan.

The South China Sea

China claims most of the South China Sea, a major shipping route with rich fishing grounds, inside a line on its maps. The Philippines, Vietnam, Malaysia, Brunei and Taiwan claim parts of the same waters. China has built artificial islands with military bases on reefs it controls.

On 12 July 2016 an international tribunal ruled, in a case brought by the Philippines, that China's historic claims had no legal basis. China rejects the ruling.

What each side wants

  • China: Control the waters and reefs it claims, using its coast guard and maritime militia more than its navy.
  • Philippines: Uphold the 2016 ruling, keep supplying its outposts such as the grounded ship at Second Thomas Shoal, and protect its fishermen. It is a US treaty ally.

Where it stands

Clashes between Chinese and Philippine vessels keep happening, with water cannon and ramming, and any one of them could draw in the United States.
